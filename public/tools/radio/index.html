Start Your Own Free Radio

The same trick that runs Alec Penn Radio, boiled down to one file. Everyone who tunes in hears the same song at the same moment, like real radio. No server. No monthly bill. No account with us.

▶ Hear the demo stationDownload the kit

  1. Put your songs online, free

    Make a free account at the Internet Archive (archive.org), then press Upload and drop in your MP3s. When it asks for a page address (the "identifier"), pick something short with no spaces, like yourname-radio. Wait until the upload has finished processing.

    Each song then lives at an address like https://archive.org/download/yourname-radio/Song Name.mp3. Only upload music you made or have the rights to.

  2. Make your song list

    Type your Archive identifier, then drop the same MP3s here. This reads each song's length on your device and writes the list for you.

  3. Fill in the kit

    Download the kit and open it in Notepad (Windows) or TextEdit (Mac). Near the top, change the name, tagline and colour. Replace the example line under SONGS with the list you just copied. Save.

    Double-click the file to try it in your browser before it goes online.

  4. Put it online, free

    Rename the file to index.html. Then, using GitHub (free):

    1. Make a free account and create a new public repository, for example my-radio.
    2. Press Add file → Upload files, drop in index.html, and press Commit changes.
    3. Open Settings → Pages. Under "Branch", pick main and press Save.
    4. In a minute or two your station is live at https://your-username.github.io/my-radio/. Share that link.

    Want your own address, like myradio.com? Domains cost roughly $10–15 a year, and GitHub Pages lets you connect one for free under the same Settings → Pages screen.

  5. Add songs later

    Upload the new songs to the same Archive item, run them through the song list maker above, add the new lines to your file, and upload it again. Listeners get the new songs the next time they open the page.

How the "live" part works

There's no broadcasting computer. The kit shuffles your songs into one fixed loop (the same order on every device) and uses the clock to work out where in that loop the station is right now. Two people opening the page at the same second land on the same song at the same spot. It costs nothing to run, and it never goes down because a computer fell asleep.

The kit is yours to change, sell or share. No credit needed.

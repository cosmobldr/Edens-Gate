Release Your Music

No label needed.

Where you can put your songs yourself, what the big stores really require, and what each option costs. Plain English, checked November 2026.

  1. Get the files ready

    • The song: a WAV file is the standard for stores. Got an MP3 only? The audio converter makes a WAV, though it can't add back quality the MP3 lost, so use your original export if you have it.
    • The cover: a square 3000×3000 picture. The cover art maker makes one in a minute. Stores reject covers with blurry images, web addresses or social handles on them.
    • The details: spell the title and your artist name exactly the same everywhere, and mark songs with swearing as explicit.

  2. Places you can upload yourself, free

    No middleman. You sign up and upload directly:

    • YouTube: upload the song as a video (the cover plus the song is enough). Free. Listing your music in YouTube Music's catalogue and collecting from other people's uploads (Content ID) comes through a distributor instead.
    • Bandcamp: free to join; fans can stream, buy and download. Bandcamp keeps 15% of digital sales (10% once you've sold $5,000 in a year) and 10% of physical sales, plus payment fees. On "Bandcamp Fridays" it waives its share; the last one of 2026 is December 4.
    • SoundCloud: free account, upload straight away, and people can follow, like and repost.
    • Internet Archive: free, permanent hosting for music you want to give away. It's where every song in Eden's Gate lives.
    • Your own station: turn those songs into a 24/7 radio with the free radio kit.

  3. Spotify, Apple Music and the big stores

    These don't take uploads from artists. Spotify tried it in 2018 and ended it in 2019, so every song on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music and TikTok arrives through a distributor. Some have free plans:

    • RouteNote Free: $0 to release; you keep 85% of what your music earns. Reviews can take a few weeks, so plan ahead.
    • SoundOn Lite (TikTok's distributor): $0 to release; you keep 90% of streaming money.
    • Paid plans (DistroKid, Amuse, RouteNote Premium and others) charge yearly or per release and let you keep 100%.

    Before you sign anything, check that you keep ownership of your songs and can leave whenever you like. Never pay anyone for "guaranteed streams": fake plays get music taken down.

  4. Release day

    • Set the release date three or four weeks out. That leaves time for review, and once your song shows up in Spotify for Artists you can pitch it to Spotify's playlist editors (at least a week before release).
    • Make a short clip for Shorts, Reels and TikTok with the song trimmer.
    • Put one link everywhere: your Bandcamp page, your station, or the distributor's free "smart link" page.

Good to know

Prices and rules change. These were checked in November 2026; read each site's current terms before you sign up. This is general information, not legal or financial advice.

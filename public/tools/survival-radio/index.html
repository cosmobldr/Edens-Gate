Survival Radio

Music when the grid goes down.

Build a solar-charged radio that plays a whole library of music with no internet, no cell signal and no power grid. Turn the knob to change channels. Every radio you build plays the same song at the same moment, like real radio. Free plans, three case designs, no account needed.

Version 1: the first radio is being built now. Parts and steps may get small fixes as it's tested.

  1. How it works

    A small computer board reads songs from a memory card and plays them through two speakers. A clock chip with its own coin battery keeps exact time for years, so the radio always knows "what's playing now," even after months in a drawer. A battery pack runs it, and a small waterproof solar panel or any USB-C charger tops it up.

    Songs go on the memory card in folders, and each folder is a channel. Need music? Everything in the Eden's Gate vault is free to download and use.

  2. Pick a case

    The wooden box plans include a cut list and full-size drilling templates you print, tape on and drill through. The printed cases print without supports; most local libraries, schools and print shops can print them if you don't have a printer.

    Wires go between the labels printed on each board.

    SD board CLK · DO · DI · CS · 3vFeather SCK · MI · MO · D10 · 3V
    Amplifier BCLK · LRC · DINFeather D9 · D6 · D5

    Feather STEMMA QT portClock board, then clock board's second port to the knob board (QT cables)

    Charging uses the charger's own USB-C port (or the solar socket). The Feather's USB-C port is only for loading the software.

  5. Load the songs

    On your computer, make one folder per channel on the memory card, like Lo-Fi, Rock, Stories, and put MP3s inside. Folder names become channel names on the screen. Tip: 128 kbps MP3s fit about twice as many songs as 320 kbps ones; the audio converter can shrink them.

    Fill it with free music: every channel in the Eden's Gate vault downloads as one zip. Unzip it into a folder on the card and it becomes a channel.

Battery safety

Use a protected lithium pack like the one listed. Don't charge it below freezing or leave it baking on a dashboard. Only the solar panel lives outside; keep the radio itself dry. A swollen or damaged battery gets replaced, never charged.
